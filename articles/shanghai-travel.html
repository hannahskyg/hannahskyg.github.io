Putting We in Weixin
By Hannah Sky Gonzalez · August 2025

Navigating the local Chinese tech space, adapting to mobile-first payments, and building cross-cultural communication skills.

Arriving in Shanghai, one of the first things that struck me was how completely digital the daily lifestyle is. Unlike the US, where cash, debit cards, or Apple Pay are common, China operates almost exclusively via WeChat (Weixin) and Alipay. Getting a local phone number, linking it to a payment gateway, and learning how to parse mini-programs for subway transit and bike rentals was a crash course in local digital adaptation.

This process forced me to look at product usability from a new perspective. Many local mini-programs are dense, highly integrated, and require a different mental model compared to West Coast app designs. Navigating these systems daily helped me better understand Chinese consumer preferences and the mechanics of super-apps like Weixin, directly informing my UX research at LOZO.

Beyond the tech, exploring Shanghai's neighborhoods—from the historical buildings of the Bund to the bustling streets of Xintiandi—was an unforgettable experience. Learning to communicate across language barriers, tasting local foods like shengjianbao, and making friends in a brand-new country taught me that stepping into difficult situations is the fastest way to grow.
